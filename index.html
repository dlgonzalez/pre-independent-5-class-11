🎤 2. Presentations: Pitch Your Company Culture

📚 Context: Applied Speaking Project

Activity: In your groups, present the company you have built to the class. Be sure to outline your roles, management style, and the core rules that govern your workplace using the structure guide below.

📋 Presentation Structure

  1. Company Profile: Name it and describe what it does.
  2. Team Roles: Who is in charge of what? Explain why you chose those roles for each person.
  3. Management Style: Traditional, cooperative, or a mix?
  4. Company Rules (Modals):
    • 3 Obligations: "Employees are required to..."
    • 2 Permissions: "Staff are allowed to..."
    • 1-2 Prohibitions: "We don't let..."
  5. Justification: Explain why this culture works for your goals.

🗣️ Help Phrases

  • Roles: My role is... We assigned [Name] as [Role] because...
  • Style: We chose a cooperative style because...
  • Obligation: Staff must... We expect everyone to...
  • Permission: Employees are allowed to... We let our team...
  • Prohibition: Staff are not allowed to... It is forbidden to...

💡 Sample B1+ Answer

"Our company is NovaTech Solutions, and we provide software consulting. In our group, Sarah is the CEO because she has great leadership skills, John is the Lead Developer, and I am the HR Manager. We've decided on a mix of cooperative and traditional management styles.

In terms of rules, all employees are required to attend a weekly sync. As the Lead Developer, John has to approve all code. Staff are allowed to choose their own working hours, and we let our developers work from home twice a week. However, staff are not allowed to share client data outside the network.

We chose this structure because we believe people work best when they have flexibility but clear guidelines."

✍️ 6. Writing: The Office Email

📚 Book Reference: Lesson 3 — Writing (Exercises A & C)

Original Instruction: "Read the office email from Tom Winters... Write an informal email replying. Give your opinions on the ideas for the new office design."

Upgraded Activity: Read the quote below from Tom Winters regarding the new office design. Write an informal reply giving your opinions. You must use parallel structure at least twice.

"So, lots of furniture, bright colors, no individual workstations. We'll still have the café, but it has changed to healthy foods... Let me know your opinions on the colors, the furniture, and what the café and rec room will be like."

- Tom Winters

📝 Steps for a Great Reply

  1. Acknowledge: Thank him for the email and the update.
  2. Share Positives: Mention 1-2 things you are excited about (e.g., the café).
  3. Raise Concerns (Politely): Give constructive feedback on what might not work (e.g., no individual desks).
  4. Grammar Check: Ensure you included at least two lists with perfect parallel structure.
  5. Sign-off: End with a warm, professional closing.

🗣️ Useful Email Phrases

Opening: Thanks for reaching out. The ideas look great!
Agreeing: I'm really excited about... I love the idea of...
Disagreeing: I have some concerns about... Would it be possible to...
Closing: Looking forward to it! Let me know what you think.
🤖

AI Co-Thinker

Need help finding the right words or structuring your email?

Help Phrases:

I really like the idea of... However, I am concerned about... Regarding the [colors/furniture]... To be productive, we need A, B, and C. (Parallel)

Sample B1+ Answer

"Hey Tom,

Thanks for the update. I love the idea of a dynamic workspace. Bright colors, comfortable furniture, and healthy food sound great!

However, I am a bit worried about having no individual workstations. To do our best work, we need to focus, communicate, and occasionally have privacy. Could we have a mix of open areas, private pods, and meeting rooms?

Let us know!
Best, [Your Name]"
